## Overview

This project investigates the presence, impact, and mitigation of algorithmic bias in latent representations generated by Electrocardiogram (ECG) Foundation Models.

Although Foundation Models can extract rich and general-purpose representations from biomedical signals, their high-dimensional embeddings may encode information unrelated to the target diagnostic task, including demographic characteristics and acquisition-specific patterns.

These latent features can act as **spurious predictive shortcuts**, potentially leading to demographic performance disparities and poor generalization across clinical institutions.

The primary objective of this work is to investigate whether bias-inducing components can be identified and selectively removed from ECG embeddings while preserving diagnostic utility, reducing dimensionality, and improving representational robustness.

The study focuses on the detection of **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)** and investigates biological sex as a sensitive demographic attribute.

## Research Objectives

The experimental framework addresses three main research questions:

1. **Bias Detection:** Do ECG Foundation Models encode demographic and acquisition-related information within their latent representations?
2. **Feature Optimization and Mitigation:** Can SHAP-guided feature selection identify and remove redundant or bias-associated components while preserving diagnostic performance?
3. **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** Do optimized and debiased representations maintain their diagnostic utility when transferred across independent clinical datasets?

## Experimental Framework

The proposed methodology is organized into three sequential phases.

### Phase I – Bias Detection

This phase characterizes demographic and domain-related information encoded in the original embeddings.

Three experimental protocols are implemented:

- **D1 – Baseline Scenario:** Evaluates diagnostic performance separately across male and female subgroups under a jointly balanced training distribution.
- **D2 – Extreme Scenario:** Introduces demographic exclusion during training to investigate shortcut learning and evaluate performance on previously unseen demographic groups.
- **D3 – Domain Shift:** Trains auxiliary classifiers to predict the originating clinical institution from embeddings, measuring the extent of acquisition-related information encoded in the latent space.

### Phase II – Feature Optimization and Bias Mitigation

This phase introduces a two-stage feature selection and purification pipeline based on SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations).

**M1 – Feature Pruning**

Identifies the most informative embedding dimensions for AF classification and progressively removes features with negligible diagnostic contributions.

The objective is to reduce dimensionality while preserving clinical predictive performance.

**M2 – Post-Pruning Feature Debiasing**

Starting from the reduced feature representation, an auxiliary classifier is trained to predict biological sex.

SHAP feature attributions are then used to identify demographic proxy features, which are progressively removed according to an exponential pruning schedule.

At each iteration, the framework evaluates:

- Diagnostic performance across demographic subgroups.
- The ability to predict the sensitive attribute from the remaining features.
- The trade-off between diagnostic utility and demographic independence.

**Random Baseline**

Both feature pruning and demographic debiasing are benchmarked against random feature elimination to assess whether improvements are attributable to SHAP-guided selection rather than dimensionality reduction alone.

### Phase III – Cross-Dataset Validation

The final phase investigates whether optimized representations generalize across independent clinical acquisition domains.

Three configurations are evaluated:

- **V1 – Full Embedding:** Baseline transfer using the original feature representation.
- **V2 – Selective Filter:** Transfers feature selection and debiasing masks derived from the source dataset to the target dataset.
- **V3 – Universal Union:** Builds a shared feature representation using the union of the features retained independently across both datasets.

These experiments assess the stability of selected features under domain shift and their ability to preserve diagnostic performance across institutions.

## Datasets

The experimental evaluation uses two independent clinical ECG databases:

| Dataset | Description |
|---|---|
| Georgia 12-Lead ECG Challenge Database | Clinical ECG recordings from a diverse United States population. |
| Chapman-Ningbo ECG Database | ECG recordings collected from two hospitals in China, providing an independent acquisition domain. |

**Diagnostic target:** Atrial Fibrillation (AF), formulated as a binary classification task.

**Sensitive attribute:** Biological sex, divided into male and female subgroups.

## Foundation Models

Two ECG Foundation Models are investigated:

| Model | Description | Embedding dimension |
|---|---|---:|
| ECG-FM | Transformer-based ECG Foundation Model | 768 |
| HuBERT-ECG | Self-supervised HuBERT adaptation for ECG signals | 768 |

The resulting embeddings are used as input to downstream classifiers without retraining the entire Foundation Models.

## Downstream Models and Explainability

Two supervised classifiers are employed to evaluate diagnostic utility and latent feature behavior:

- **Logistic Regression:** Linear classification baseline.
- **Random Forest:** Non-linear ensemble classifier.

Feature attribution is performed using the SHAP library:

- `LinearExplainer` for Logistic Regression.
- `TreeExplainer` for Random Forest.

## Evaluation Metrics

The framework evaluates both diagnostic performance and demographic fairness.

**Clinical performance:**
- Accuracy
- F1-Score
- Macro F1-Score
- AUROC

**Fairness and demographic bias:**
- Maximum accuracy gap between demographic subgroups.
- True Positive Rate (TPR) ratio.
- False Positive Rate (FPR) ratio.
- Adversarial classification accuracy for sensitive attribute prediction.

## Main Results

The experiments highlight several important findings.

### 1. Demographic Information in Latent Representations

Both Foundation Models encode information associated with biological sex, resulting in performance disparities across demographic subgroups.

Under demographic exclusion experiments, performance degradation is particularly evident when evaluating previously unseen populations, supporting the presence of demographic shortcut learning.

### 2. Acquisition-Related Bias

Both models encode substantial information about the originating clinical institution.

In the domain identification experiment, HuBERT-ECG achieves nearly perfect classification accuracy, while ECG-FM also exhibits strong domain separability.

This indicates that latent representations retain acquisition-specific characteristics that may affect cross-institutional generalization.

### 3. SHAP-Guided Feature Optimization

The M1 experiments demonstrate that substantial dimensionality reduction is possible without necessarily sacrificing diagnostic utility.

In particular, reducing the original 768-dimensional representation to **68 features** preserves diagnostic performance for ECG-FM and HuBERT-ECG substantially better than random feature elimination.

### 4. Demographic Bias Mitigation

The M2 experiments demonstrate that targeted removal of demographic proxy features can reduce sensitive attribute predictability while retaining much of the original diagnostic performance.

The selected operating points are:

| Foundation Model | Features removed during M2 |
|---|---:|
| ECG-FM | 32 |
| HuBERT-ECG | 16 |

These results highlight the trade-off between demographic independence and diagnostic utility.

### 5. Cross-Dataset Generalization

The cross-dataset experiments reveal different generalization behaviors between the two Foundation Models.

- **ECG-FM** demonstrates comparatively strong cross-dataset generalization across the evaluated configurations.
- **HuBERT-ECG** exhibits substantial sensitivity to acquisition domain, with severe performance degradation when transferring from Chapman-Ningbo to Georgia.

Overall, the findings emphasize that evaluating diagnostic performance on an internal dataset alone is insufficient to establish the reliability of ECG representations.

## Future Work

Several directions could extend this research:

- **Knowledge Distillation:** Transfer purified representations to compact student models suitable for resource-constrained medical devices and edge computing.
- **Additional Clinical Targets:** Extend the evaluation to other cardiac pathologies to investigate whether the observed bias patterns generalize beyond AF.
- **Additional Sensitive Attributes:** Investigate age, ethnicity, and intersectional demographic characteristics.
- **Multi-Center Validation:** Expand the evaluation to additional clinical institutions, acquisition devices, and recording protocols.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Explore dedicated alignment techniques to mitigate acquisition-related bias while preserving diagnostic information.
